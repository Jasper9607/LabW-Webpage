Research

Computational frameworks for immune states, T cell recognition and disease mechanisms.

Lab W develops single-cell and spatial omics technologies, computational TCR immunology methods, and integrative frameworks to decode immune states, T cell recognition, and autoimmune disease mechanisms.

01

Single-cell and Spatial Omics Technologies

Mapping cell states and tissue context across modalities.

Key question

How can single-cell and spatial omics data reveal tissue microenvironments, disease-associated cell states and cross-sample variation?

Approach

We integrate single-cell omics, spatial transcriptomics, spatial metabolomics, cross-sample alignment and interpretable tissue microenvironment modeling.

Representative outputs

Algorithms, analysis frameworks, disease atlases and reusable data resources for computational and experimental collaborators.

Recruitment interests

Students and collaborators interested in spatial biology, omics integration and biomedical data science are welcome.

02

Core Focus

Computational TCR Immunology

Key question

How can TCR repertoire signals, T cell states and antigen recognition be connected into robust, interpretable immune maps?

Approach

We study TCR repertoire analysis, TCR specificity prediction, TCR alpha-beta reference construction, immune receptor databases and clonotype-transcriptome integration.

Representative outputs

Reference resources, computational benchmarks, immune receptor databases and models linking T cell states with antigen recognition.

Recruitment interests

We are especially interested in people working on computational immunology, machine learning for immune repertoires and experimental validation.

03

Autoimmune Disease Systems Biology

Connecting immune cell states, clonal expansion and clinical context.

Key question

Which immune cell states and T cell clonal patterns are associated with autoimmune disease mechanisms and patient heterogeneity?

Approach

We combine clinical sample analysis, multi-omics integration, T cell clonal expansion analysis and computational discovery with experimental follow-up.

Representative outputs

Systems-level disease maps, candidate mechanisms and collaboration-ready analytical frameworks.

Recruitment interests

We welcome backgrounds in immunology, clinical medicine, statistics, computer science and molecular biology.
